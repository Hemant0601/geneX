2. **Measurement again 30 days after go-live**, same method.
3. **Permission to publish the numbers**, **named or anonymised** as the client prefers (anonymised is the default). The client sees the case study before it goes out.

## 4. Selection criteria

**Must-haves (all yes, or it isn't a founding slot):**
- [ ] In niche: a **real-estate broker, small brokerage or channel partner in India**, a **broker in Dubai**, or a **private clinic** (dental, derm/aesthetic, physio; 1–10 doctors).
- [ ] About **40–500 enquiries a month** from **2 or more sources** (under about 20 a month is too few to measure).
- [ ] WhatsApp is **answered by hand** today.
- [ ] Can give **admin/partner access** to the Meta business portfolio and open a **WhatsApp provider account in the business's name**.
- [ ] Comfortable with the numbers being **published, named or anonymised**.
- [ ] Has time for the kickoff, demo, handover and feedback interview.
- [ ] The decision-maker is on the call (or confirms in writing).
- [ ] Not asking for anything out of scope (bulk/cold broadcasts, general-purpose bots).

**Tie-breakers (when more than one applicant fits the same slot):**
1. **Lane balance:** keep the 2 RE + 1 clinic target; max 1 Dubai.
2. **Measurability:** 100–500 enquiries a month beats 40–100 (bigger n).
3. **Source mix:** a portal + Meta ads (or missed calls + bookings for clinics) shows the full system.
4. **Readiness:** access can be complete within 5 working days of signing.
5. **Capacity fit:** the start date fits **max 2 Sprints at once**.
6. Willing to be **named** (a bonus, never a requirement).

**Mix rule (suggestion, not in the strategy):** if no suitable clinic has applied by **Mon 23 Nov 2026**, the third slot may go to a second-best real-estate applicant. Decide and note it; don't hold a slot open past the close date.

## 6. Measurement protocol: baseline → 30 days

### 6.1 Definitions (same as the case study, [`08-case-study-template-and-consent.md`](./08-case-study-template-and-consent.md) §1)
- **Lead:** a new enquiry from a connected source, after removing obvious spam, test leads and exact duplicates (same person, same source, within 24 hours). **Write the exclusion rule down before counting**, and apply it to both periods.
- **Arrival time:** when the lead **reached the system**: Meta lead created time; portal alert email received time; Instagram DM received time; website form submit time; missed-call time.
- **First-reply time:** when the first human or automated reply was **sent to that lead** (WhatsApp, call, DM).
- **Metrics:** median first-reply time · % answered within 5 minutes · % qualified · bookings per 100 leads · no-show rate · owner-reported hours saved a week (**self-reported**).

### 6.2 Baseline (during the build, before go-live)
**Window:** from Full Access to the day before go-live (about 10 working days). If fewer than about 30 leads arrive, also use the client's own records from the 2–4 weeks before signing, **if they have timestamps** (Meta Leads Center export, portal dashboard), and mark that part "from client records".

**How to capture it without changing how leads are handled:**
1. **Shadow capture (arrival):** on day 1–2, connect each source to the lead log **in log-only mode** (no auto-reply): the automation writes arrival time, source and a lead ID. Nothing is sent to the lead.
2. **First reply (by the team):** add a `first_reply_at` column to the log; the person who replies fills it in (or taps a one-field form) when they first reply or call. Owner spot-checks 10 entries against their WhatsApp timestamps each week. No chat screenshots needed.
3. **Qualified / booked / showed:** the team marks these columns as they happen; for clinics, from the appointment book.
4. **Audit test enquiries** (with consent, up to 3 channels) are reported separately as "timed tests", not mixed into the baseline.
5. **Seasonality note:** if the baseline includes **Diwali week (around Fri 6 – Tue 10 Nov 2026)** or a project launch, record it and say so next to the numbers.

### 6.3 After (30 days from go-live)
- **Window:** go-live day + 30 calendar days. The system logs arrival and first-reply times automatically.
- **Same sources, same exclusion rule, same definitions.** If a source is added or dropped, report it separately.
- **Human review:** Hemant reads the **first 100 AI conversations**. Log each issue in a `review` tab: conversation ID, what went wrong, fix applied, date. This feeds "what didn't work".
- Weekly 10-minute check with the owner (WhatsApp is fine): anything odd, any opt-out spikes.

### 6.4 Measurement sheet (one per client, in the client's Google account)
`lead_id · source · arrived_at · first_reply_at · minutes_to_first_reply · replied_by (human/system) · qualified (y/n) · booked (y/n) · booking_at · showed (y/n/na) · opted_out (y/n) · excluded (y/n + reason) · period (baseline/after)`

## 9. If something goes wrong

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| The client stops filling the baseline column | Use the shadow-capture arrival times and whatever reply times exist; report the gap honestly ("first-reply time available for n of N leads") |
| The client withdraws publication permission before publishing | Nothing is published; the price stays the same (Schedule E item 9) |
| Results are weak or mixed | Publish honestly (with "what didn't work") or not at all. Never cherry-pick a good week |
| The client churns before day 30 | Publish nothing beyond what they agree to; no partial "results" |
| Delivery slips past 10 working days (your side) | First month of Lead Desk Care free (on-time promise); say so the day it happens |
| Two founding Sprints overlap with a full-price one | Don't start a third Sprint: stagger start dates (max 2 at once) |
